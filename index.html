Unit 6: Database Design and Normalisation

My original proposal concerned a custodian-bank CLO database with entities for deals, assets, trades and portfolio tests. The proposed data proved too large and complex for me to manage effectively, so I independently changed to a smaller Olist e-commerce sample. In practical database work I examined 1,000 customers, 1,000 delivered orders, 1,151 order items and 942 products using SQLite. Primary and foreign keys linked the entities; SQL analysis returned an average merchandise order value of 135.15. This change of scope was a pragmatic response to feasibility, although earlier data profiling would have reduced rework.

Evaluation of the Final Project Against the Unit 6 Proposal

The Unit 6 proposal aimed to model Collateralised Loan Obligations for a custodian-bank Corporate Trust context, including collateral managers, obligors, assets, trades and portfolio tests. It envisaged Python, SQLite and Power BI to support structured analysis. I found the proposed banking dataset too large and difficult to work with, and independently selected a smaller Olist e-commerce sample. This was a significant change of domain, not simply a change of dataset size: the CLO-specific portfolio tests were not implemented.

The revised project retained the transferable aims of relational modelling, controlled preparation, SQL analysis and independent validation. Four tables—Customers, Orders, Products and Order Items—used keys and joins to separate transactional facts from descriptive attributes. Staging and integrity checks supported a final database with no reported foreign-key violations. The individual executive summary further documented Python profiling, nine validation checks and an interactive HTML dashboard; the earlier database design report had described an intelligent dashboard as future work, so the two reports represent different stages of development.

The sample of 1,000 delivered orders produced merchandise sales of 135,150.38 and average order value of 135.15, excluding freight. The highest-sales category was beleza_saude (15,187.21), São Paulo led the states (51,137.61), and November 2017 led the months (12,312.79). These are findings for a selected, non-random sample, not evidence of business-wide performance. Restricting the analysis to delivered orders introduces further selection bias.

My strongest outcome was delivering a validated, manageable database after encountering an impractical initial scope. A weakness was insufficient early assessment of data volume and complexity. Further work should test representative samples, preserve postal-code prefixes as text, extend the schema to sellers and document recovery testing. AI-assisted suggestions were useful for SQL and troubleshooting, but the outputs required manual verification.

Project evidence: Initial proposal · Individual executive summary

Professional and Personal Development

This module strengthened my SQL and SQLite knowledge, relational modelling, data cleaning, analytical interpretation and awareness of data protection. The Olist project made abstract concepts tangible: keys and normalisation improved structure, while validation checks were needed before interpreting totals. Working independently required prioritisation and accountability. My initial choice of a difficult banking dataset showed that ambition must be balanced against time, available data and technical capability.

Peer discussions developed my written communication and critical evaluation. Comparing GDPR and UAE PDPL, then responding to comments about pseudonymisation and security controls, encouraged me to distinguish legal requirements from practical implementation. I also became more critical of AI assistance: generated queries can accelerate exploration but do not replace evidence-based testing.

My development priorities are to profile datasets before committing to a design, strengthen Python automation and SQL optimisation, practise with larger representative samples and document backup/recovery procedures. I will use validation results and clearly stated limitations to support future decisions.
